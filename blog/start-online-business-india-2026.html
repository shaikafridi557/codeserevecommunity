How to Start an Online Business in India in 2026 (Step-by-Step Guide)

Everything you need to go from idea to first online order — the business model, registration and GST, website, payments, delivery and marketing — with honest costs for 2026.

How do you start an online business in India?

Pick a business model and test demand, register the business (Udyam for a proprietorship, or an LLP / private limited company), get GST if your sales or model require it, open a current account, build a website or marketplace store, connect a payment gateway such as Razorpay or Cashfree, set up shipping or delivery, publish the legal policies, and then market the store with SEO, Google Business Profile, social media and ads. A basic online store can launch in 3–6 weeks for roughly ₹30,000–₹80,000 in first-year costs.

1. Choose your online business model

"Online business" covers very different businesses. The model you pick decides your costs, your registrations and how you will find customers.

ModelExamplesStarting costMain challenge
D2C brand (own products)Clothing, skincare, snacks, handicraftsMedium–high (stock + packaging)Product quality, repeat customers
Local shop going onlineGrocery, pharmacy, bakery, electronics in your areaLow–mediumFast local delivery, WhatsApp orders
ServicesTuition, consulting, design, repairs, clinicsLowTrust, reviews, booking flow
Digital productsCourses, templates, e-books, softwareLowMarketing and content
Reselling / dropshippingSourcing from suppliers and selling onlineLow–mediumThin margins, delivery control

For most first-time founders, a service business or a local shop going online is the lowest-risk start, because you can get paying customers before investing in stock.

2. Validate demand before you spend

Many online businesses fail because nobody was searching for what they sell. Before you build anything, spend one week checking demand:

  • Google search: type your product or service with your city ("cake delivery Kukatpally", "tuition for class 10 KPHB") and look at who appears, how many reviews they have and what they charge.
  • Google Keyword Planner (free inside Google Ads) and Google Trends show rough search volume and whether interest is rising or falling.
  • Marketplaces: check Amazon, Flipkart or Meesho for similar products — number of reviews is a good proxy for demand.
  • Pre-sell: share photos or a simple WhatsApp catalogue with 50 people in your target group and take 5–10 real orders before scaling up.
Rule of thumb: if you cannot get your first 10 customers from friends, WhatsApp groups and local word of mouth, a website alone will not fix it. Fix the offer first.

3. Register your business

You can start testing as an individual, but you will need a registered business to open a current account, get a payment gateway approved and look trustworthy to customers.

  • Sole proprietorship + Udyam registration: the simplest and cheapest option for one owner. Udyam (MSME) registration is free on the government portal and helps with bank accounts and schemes.
  • LLP: good for two or more partners who want limited liability without the compliance of a company.
  • Private limited company: best if you plan to raise investment or grow a larger team; higher yearly compliance.
  • One Person Company (OPC): a company structure for a single founder.

Also plan for a current bank account in the business name, a business PAN (for LLP or company), and any industry licence — for example an FSSAI registration for food products, or a drug licence for medicines. A local CA can register most of these within one to three weeks.

4. GST for online sellers

GST rules for online businesses change from time to time, so always confirm your case with a CA. The broad picture in 2026:

  • The general registration threshold is ₹40 lakh annual turnover for goods and ₹20 lakh for services in most states (lower in some special-category states).
  • Selling through marketplaces such as Amazon or Flipkart has historically needed GST registration, with some relaxations for small sellers in recent years — check the current rule for your category and whether you sell within or across states.
  • Selling goods to customers in other states can trigger compulsory registration even below the threshold, depending on current exemptions.
  • Selling on your own website to customers in your own state below the threshold usually does not require GST — but many businesses register anyway so they can claim input tax credit and work with B2B buyers.

Once registered, your website must show your GSTIN on invoices and charge the right tax rate per product. A proper GST billing setup saves hours at filing time.

5. Website or marketplace — or both?

Own websiteMarketplace (Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho)
TrafficYou bring it (SEO, ads, social)Built-in buyers
FeesGateway fee ~2% per transactionCommission + fees, often 10–30%+
Customer dataYours — you can remarket on WhatsApp/emailMostly stays with the marketplace
BrandFull control of design and storyLimited; you compete on price beside others
Best forBrands, services, repeat customers, local businessTesting products, quick first sales

The strongest approach for most Indian brands is both: use a marketplace for discovery and your own website for repeat customers, better margins and your brand.

For the website itself, Shopify is quickest for pure product stores, WooCommerce (WordPress) gives more control and lower monthly cost, and a custom build makes sense for bookings, subscriptions or unusual workflows. We compare these in WordPress vs custom website.

Whatever you choose, make sure the site has: fast mobile pages, clear prices, real product photos, a WhatsApp button, visible contact details and address, and SEO basics (titles, descriptions, product schema, sitemap).

6. Payment gateway setup

Popular payment gateways in India include Razorpay, Cashfree, PayU, PhonePe PG and CCAvenue. They all support UPI, cards, net banking and wallets. Standard domestic pricing is typically around 2% per transaction, with lower rates sometimes available for UPI and negotiated rates at higher volumes.

To get approved quickly, gateways usually check that your website has:

  • A working domain (not a "coming soon" page) with real products or services and prices
  • Contact page with phone, email and business address
  • Privacy policy, terms & conditions, refund/cancellation policy and shipping/delivery policy
  • KYC documents: PAN, bank account proof, business registration and owner ID

Also enable Cash on Delivery carefully — it raises conversions in India but also returns. Many stores allow COD only below a certain order value or charge a small COD fee.

7. Shipping, delivery and returns

  • Shipping aggregators such as Shiprocket, NimbusPost or Delhivery's self-serve platform let you compare couriers, print labels and track parcels from one dashboard.
  • India Post is often the cheapest option for small, non-urgent parcels to smaller towns.
  • Local delivery (your own staff or apps like Porter / Dunzo-style services) works well for food, groceries and same-day orders in one city.
  • Send order and shipping updates on WhatsApp — customers read them far more than emails. See our guide to WooCommerce order notifications on WhatsApp.

8. Policies and legal pages

India's Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 expect online sellers to show clear seller details, prices with taxes, return and refund terms, and a grievance contact. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 also applies when you collect customer names, phone numbers and addresses. At minimum, publish:

  1. Privacy policy (what data you collect and why)
  2. Terms & conditions
  3. Refund, return and cancellation policy
  4. Shipping / delivery policy
  5. Contact page with legal business name, address, phone, email and grievance officer details

Templates are a starting point; ask a lawyer or CA to review them for your exact business.

9. SEO for a new online business

SEO is the cheapest long-term source of customers, but it starts on day one, not after launch.

  • One page per product category and per service, each targeting a real search phrase ("handmade jute bags online", "home tuition in Kukatpally").
  • Unique product descriptions — never copy the manufacturer's text that 50 other sites use.
  • Structured data: add Product schema (price, availability, reviews) for products and LocalBusiness schema with your address for local businesses.
  • Google Business Profile: essential if customers can visit you or you deliver locally. Follow our Google Business Profile guide.
  • Speed: compress images and keep the theme light — see how to pass Core Web Vitals.
  • Search Console: verify your domain, submit the sitemap and check indexing every week.
  • Reviews: ask every happy customer for a Google review. Review count is one of the strongest local trust signals — and AI search tools use it too.

Frequently asked questions

Can I start an online business in India without GST?

Yes, in many cases. Service businesses and sellers who sell only on their own website within their state can usually start without GST until they cross the turnover threshold (₹20 lakh for services, ₹40 lakh for goods in most states). Marketplace selling and inter-state sales of goods have different rules, so confirm your case with a CA.

How much money do I need to start an online business in India?

A small service business or online store can start for roughly ₹30,000 to ₹80,000 in first-year costs for registration, domain, hosting, website and initial marketing, not including stock. Digital product businesses can start for less.

Which is better for beginners: Shopify or WooCommerce?

Shopify is quicker to set up and needs less maintenance, but has a monthly fee and app costs. WooCommerce on WordPress has lower running costs and more flexibility, but needs good hosting and regular updates. For most small Indian stores either works; choose based on budget and how much control you want.

Do I need a company to get a payment gateway?

No. Most Indian payment gateways accept sole proprietors with a current account, PAN and a registration such as Udyam or GST. You also need a live website with contact details and policy pages.

How long does it take to start selling online?

With a clear plan, 3 to 6 weeks is realistic: one to three weeks for registration and bank account, and one to three weeks for the website, payment gateway approval and shipping setup, often running in parallel.
